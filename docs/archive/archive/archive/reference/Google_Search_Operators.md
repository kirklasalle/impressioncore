Google Search Operators: A Comprehensive List
Google search operators are powerful tools that allow you to refine your search queries and get more precise results. They are special commands that you can add to your search terms to dictate how Google interprets your query. 
Here's a breakdown of common Google search operators, categorized by their function:
Basic Operators:
Operator 	Function	Example
"" (Quotation marks)	Searches for the exact phrase within the quotes, excluding synonyms.	"tallest building"
OR (or pipe symbol	)	Searches for results containing either of the terms joined by OR or
AND	Searches for results containing all the terms joined by AND.	B2B AND B2C

- (Minus sign)	Excludes a specific term or phrase from the results.	dog breeds -terriers
* (Asterisk)	Acts as a wildcard, representing any word or phrase in that position.	car * career

() (Parentheses)	Groups multiple terms or operators to control the search order.	(B2B OR B2C) marketing jobs
$	Searches for prices.	piano $1500
define:	Displays the definition of a word or phrase.	define:neuroscience

Advanced Google Search Operators:
Advanced search operators, also called search commands, are special terms or symbols you can include in your Google search query to refine your results and find information more precisely than a standard text search. 
Here are some commonly used advanced Google Search Operators:

1. site: 

Function: Limits your search results to a specific website or domain.
Example: site:wikipedia.org "Nikola Tesla" - This will search for results about "Nikola Tesla" specifically within the Wikipedia website. 

2. filetype: or ext: 

Function: Filters results to include only specific file types (e.g., PDF, DOC, PPT).
Example: filetype:pdf "marketing strategy" - This will search for PDF files containing the phrase "marketing strategy". You can also use ext: with the file extension. 

3. intitle: 

Function: Searches for pages that have a specific keyword or phrase in the title of the page.
Example: intitle:"SEO techniques" - This will show pages with "SEO techniques" in their title. 

4. allintitle: 

Function: Searches for pages that contain all the specified terms in the title tag.
Example: allintitle: "gearbox transmission" - This will show pages with both "gearbox" and "transmission" in their titles. 

5. inurl: 

Function: Searches for results that include a specific keyword or phrase in the URL.
Example: inurl:blog "SEO tips" - This will find pages with "blog" in the URL and "SEO tips" in the content. 

6. allinurl: 

Function: Searches for pages that contain all the specified terms in the URL.
Example: allinurl:amazon field-keywords nikon - This will search the URL for all terms following "allinurl". 

7. intext: 

Function: Searches for pages that contain a specific word or phrase in the body text of the document.
Example: intext:"orbi vs eero vs google wifi" - This will show pages with that phrase in the text. 

8. allintext: 

Function: Searches for pages that contain all the specified terms in their content.
Example: allintext: "samsung galaxy" - This will show pages with both "samsung" and "galaxy" in their content. 

9. AROUND(X) 

Function: Finds pages where two specified terms appear within a certain number of words (X) of each other in the text.
Example: Apple AROUND(3) phone - This will show websites where "Apple" and "phone" appear within three words of each other. 

10. define:
- Function: Provides a definition of a word or phrase.
- Example: define:algorithm - This will display a dictionary definition of "algorithm". 
11. cache:
- Function: Shows you the most recent cached (archived) version of a website.
- Example: cache:symphonicdigital.com - This will show a previous version of the Symphonic Digital website. 
12. related:
- Function: Helps you find websites that are similar to a specific website.
- Example: related:symphonicdigital.com - This will bring up similar sites to Symphonic Digital's website. 
13. source:
- Function: Finds news articles from a specific news source in Google News.
- Example: climate change source:BBC - This will show news articles about "climate change" specifically from the BBC. 
14. before: and after:
- Function: Limits your search results to pages published before or after a specific date.
- Example: Microsoft before:2010-05-08 or Microsoft after:2010-05-08. 
15. inanchor:
- Function: Searches for pages that have backlinks with a specific anchor text.
- Example: inanchor:"tesla announcements" - This will show pages with backlinks using "tesla announcements" as anchor text. 
16. allinanchor:
- Function: Searches for pages that have backlinks with all the specified terms in the anchor text.
- Example: allinanchor:"tesla announcements". 
17. weather:
- Function: Searches for the weather in a specific location.
- Example: weather:New Jersey. 
18. stocks:
- Function: Searches for stock information for a given company or ticker symbol.
- Example: stocks:nvidia. 
19. map:
- Function: Forces Google to show map results for a location.
- Example: map:Manhattan. 

Note: Google sometimes updates or removes search operators, so it's always good to verify their functionality. Some of the operators that were previously functional but are now unreliable or deprecated include AROUND(X), loc:, location:, daterange:, ~, +, inpostauthor:, allinpostauthor:, inposttitle:, link:, info:, and phonebook:. 
By combining these advanced operators, you can create highly refined search queries that help you find exactly what you're looking for.
